IPv6 in Depth

The fundamentals posts get IPv6 working. This series covers why it behaves the way it does once it's running: the packets behind SLAAC, the lease behind your prefix, the ICMPv6 your firewall must let through, and what routing does differently. Every post has RouterOS and IOS side by side.

IPv6 NDP DHCPv6-PD RFC 4890 OSPFv3 BGP

Most IPv6 problems in small and mid-size networks aren't exotic. Phones with no address. A prefix that disappears after a week. A firewall that is either too open or quietly breaks path MTU. An OSPFv3 adjacency that won't form for no visible reason. Each one comes from a mechanism the fundamentals skip. These posts go one level down and stop there: enough to reason about the failure and fix it, with configs for both vendors.

Start with the fundamentals if v6 is new to you: MikroTik or Cisco IOS. To roll it out on a live network, read Adding IPv6 Without Breaking IPv4. Posts are filed under the ipv6 category.
