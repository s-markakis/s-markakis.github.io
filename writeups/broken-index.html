Broken by Design

Every network fails in a handful of classic ways, and each one has a shape you can learn to recognise on sight. This series walks the most common failures — what the user sees, why it happens, an animated diagram of the mechanism, how to catch it in thirty seconds, and how to stop it coming back.

Broken by Design Failure Modes Troubleshooting Animated Diagrams

The architecture guides show how to build networks right; this series shows how they go wrong. Each post takes one failure mode that every network engineer meets eventually and dissects it: the symptom as the user reports it, the mechanism with an animated diagram, the field check to confirm it in seconds, and the fix. The companion series, Surgery on a Running Network, is how you change a live network without causing these.

They are filed under the broken-by-design category on the blog.

Addressing & Services

DHCP and DNS are invisible until they fail, and then nothing new works — or everything goes slow.

06No One Can Get an AddressNew devices get a useless 169.254 address while everything already online keeps working.
